── Part 5: Why ZKP — Not Approximate Location? ──

                        ZKP (Plonky2)             Approx. Location
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Privacy            Exact coords stay         Coarsened but reveals
                       private (zero-knowledge)     approximate area

    Verifiability      Cryptographic proof       Trust-the-server
                       (anyone can verify)          (no proof, just assertion)

    Auditability       Proof can live on a       No proof to store
                       public ledger forever        or audit later

    Tamper-proof       Circuit rejects wrong     Approx. location can be
                       inputs (idhash mismatch)     fabricated trivially

    Device binding     idhash = SHA-256(AK)      No hardware binding
                       → TPM-rooted                 → software-only

    Why Plonky2 specifically?
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    No trusted setup     Transparent — no ceremony, no toxic waste
                         Anyone can verify without trusting a setup phase

    Hash-based           Commitments use Poseidon hash (no elliptic curves)
                         → Post-quantum resistant (PQC-ready)

    Fast                 ~50ms proof generation, ~100ms verification
                         Recursive proof composition for complex policies

    CPU-native           64-bit prime field (Goldilocks)
                         → Maps to native CPU instructions, no BigInt overhead

    Bottom line: Approximate location is privacy theatre —
    it obscures from humans but not from algorithms.
    ZKP provides mathematical privacy with cryptographic verifiability.
